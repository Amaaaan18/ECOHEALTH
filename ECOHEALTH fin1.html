Science & Evidence

Body Mass Index (BMI) & Metric Formulation

Verified source: CDC
CDC Adult BMI CalculatorCenters for Disease Control and Prevention. Official calculator and category definitions for adults 20 and older.
BMI = Weight (kg) / [Height (m)]²

Calculation breakdown

1. Height (m) = Height (cm) / 100
2. Height squared = [Height (m)] × [Height (m)]
3. BMI = Weight (kg) / Height squared, rounded to 1 decimal place
Example: 70 kg, 170 cm gives 1.70 m, then 1.70 × 1.70 = 2.89, then 70 / 2.89 = 24.2 kg/m².

Standard clinical thresholds (CDC / WHO) vs revised Asian-Indian

CategoryCDC / WHO standardRevised Asian-Indian
Underweight< 18.5 kg/m²< 18.5 kg/m²
Healthy Weight18.5 – 24.9 kg/m²18.5 – 22.9 kg/m²
Overweight25.0 – 29.9 kg/m²23.0 – 24.9 kg/m²
Obese≥ 30.0 kg/m²≥ 25.0 kg/m²

BMI uses only weight and height, so it cannot separate lean muscle from fat or show where fat is stored. For this reason the station pairs it with the Waist-to-Height Ratio (WHtR = Waist cm / Height cm) and the Waist-to-Hip Ratio (card 3). Metabolic risk rises at lower BMI in Asian Indians, hence the lower revised cutoffs.

Resting Metabolic Rate (RMR/BMR) & Energy Expenditure

Peer-reviewed: PubMed Central
Stubelj M, Poklar Vatovec T. Predicting Equations and Resting Energy Expenditure Changes in Overweight Adults (PMC7478086)Slovenian Journal of Public Health, 2020. Compares measured resting energy expenditure (indirect calorimetry) with estimates from the Mifflin-St Jeor, Harris-Benedict and other equations.

Mifflin-St Jeor equations

Men: BMR = (10 × W) + (6.25 × H) − (5 × A) + 5
Women: BMR = (10 × W) + (6.25 × H) − (5 × A) − 161
W = weight (kg), H = height (cm), A = age (years). Result in kcal/day. Example, man, 70 kg, 170 cm, age 30: 700 + 1062.5 − 150 + 5 = 1,617.5 kcal.

BMR/RMR is the baseline energy your cells use at complete physiological rest. The cited study found that for women the equation estimates differed significantly from measured values, so treat the result as an estimate (individual values can differ by roughly 10 percent), not a measurement.

Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)

TDEE = BMR × Activity Multiplier
Activity levelMultiplier
Sedentary1.200
Lightly Active1.375
Moderately Active1.550
Very Active1.725
Example: 1,617.5 × 1.550 = about 2,507 kcal/day.

Central Adiposity: Waist-to-Hip Ratio (WHR) & Waist Circumference

Peer-reviewed: PubMed Central
Comparison of Waist Circumference and Waist-to-Hip Ratio in Assessing Abdominal Obesity and Cardiometabolic Risk Among Women Aged 15-49 Years in India (PMC13286478)National cross-sectional survey of Indian women comparing waist circumference and WHR as measures of abdominal obesity.
WHR = Waist Circumference (cm) / Hip Circumference (cm)
Example: waist 90 cm, hip 100 cm gives WHR = 0.90. Measure the waist at the narrowest point (or midway between lowest rib and hip bone) and the hip at the widest point.

Clinical risk thresholds (WHO criteria)

MeasureMenWomen
WHR: substantially increased metabolic riskWaist-to-hip ratio> 0.90> 0.85
Waist circumference: South Asian / Asian-Indian action pointConsensus cutoff for abdominal obesity> 90 cm> 80 cm
Waist circumference: WHO action levelsLevel 1 / Level 2≥ 94 / ≥ 102 cm≥ 80 / ≥ 88 cm

Clinical rationale: BMI cannot show where fat sits. Waist-based measures help separate subcutaneous fat (under the skin) from visceral intra-abdominal fat, the deeper fat linked to insulin resistance and cardiovascular strain. Cutoffs vary by sex, age and ethnicity, and WHR and waist circumference can classify the same person differently, so use them as screening tools alongside BMI.

Circadian & Ultradian Sleep Architecture

90-minute sleep cycle formula

Total Sleep = (N × 90 min) + 15 min latency
Wake time = Bedtime + 15 min + (N × 1.5 h)
Bedtime  = Wake time − 15 min − (N × 1.5 h)
N = number of full cycles (4 to 6 suit most adults). Example: bedtime 11:00 PM and N = 5 gives 11:00 PM + 15 min + 7 h 30 min = 6:45 AM.

Ultradian stages (share of the night)

N1NREM-2NREM-3REM
StageShareRole
NREM-1~5%Light transition into sleep
NREM-2~45 – 55%Sleep spindles and motor memory
NREM-3~15 – 25%Slow-wave deep sleep and growth hormone repair
REM~20 – 25%Rapid eye movement and cognitive integration

Preventing sleep inertia

Ending sleep at a cycle boundary means waking from light sleep (NREM-1/2 or REM) instead of being pulled out of deep NREM-3, which helps prevent sleep inertia (morning grogginess). The 90-minute length is an average (about 70 to 110 minutes), so treat the times as a planning guide.

Formulas Used

1. Body Mass Index (BMI)
BMI = weight (kg) / [height (m)]^2
height (m) = height (cm) / 100
Result in kg/m², rounded to 1 decimal. Revised Asian-Indian cutoffs: under 18.5 underweight, 18.5 to 22.9 healthy, 23.0 to 24.9 overweight, 25.0 or more obese (global WHO healthy range: 18.5 to 24.9).
2. Resting Calories (Mifflin-St Jeor)
Men: BMR = 10W + 6.25H - 5A + 5
Women: BMR = 10W + 6.25H - 5A - 161
W = weight (kg), H = height (cm), A = age (years). Result in kcal/day.
3. Total Daily Calorie Burn
Total = Resting Calories x Activity (1.2 / 1.375 / 1.55 / 1.725)
4. Waist vs Height Check (WHtR)
WHtR = waist (cm) / height (cm)
Keep your waist below half your height; 0.50 or more is a screening sign of extra belly fat.
5. Waist-to-Hip Ratio (WHR)
WHR = waist (cm) / hip (cm)
Increased risk (WHO): men above 0.90, women above 0.85.
6. MET Exercise Energy
kcal = MET x weight (kg) x time (hours)
Net kcal = (MET - 1) x weight (kg) x time (hours)
7. 90-Minute Sleep Cycle
Wake time = Bedtime + 15 min + (n x 90 min), n = 3 to 6
The 15 minutes allows for the time it takes to fall asleep.
Health Tip: Drink 350-500 mL of water immediately upon waking to wake up your body.
